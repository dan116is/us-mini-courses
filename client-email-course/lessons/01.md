# Lesson 1 — Invoice emails that AP can pay

**Time:** 40 minutes to set the template; 10 minutes each invoice after  
**Output:** One invoice-email template + a day-7 nudge + a field list that matches your invoice PDF.

## The real audience

Your client is not the only reader. **Accounts payable** (or the office manager, or the owner’s spouse) needs: who you are, what job, which PO, amount, due date, how to pay. Cute writing slows payment.

## Before the email: the invoice itself

Whatever tool you use (Wave, QuickBooks, a numbered PDF), the email cannot fix a vague invoice. Include:

- Your legal name / DBA as the client knows you
- Invoice number (never reuse)
- Client’s legal name and **billing email** (not the marketing intern’s Gmail unless they said so)
- PO number if they issued one (US mid-market will sit on invoices without it)
- Job name + period of work
- Line items that match the signed scope, not nicknames
- Amount due, currency USD, due date
- How to pay (ACH instructions, card link, or check address — only methods you actually watch)

If you do not have terms in writing yet, pick one, put it on the invoice, and use it going forward (many small US shops use due upon receipt or Net 15). Changing terms mid-relationship needs a heads-up, not a surprise on the PDF.

This is not legal advice; if you need collections counsel, stop emailing and call one.

## Email 1 — Issue

**Subject:** `Invoice [####] — [Client] — [Job or month] — due [Mon D, YYYY]`

**To:** billing contact  
**Cc:** your day-to-day client contact if they are not AP  
**Attach:** PDF (even if the tool also has a link)

```
Hi [Name],

Attached is invoice [####] for [job / January retainer], amount $[X]. Due [date].

PO: [number or “none on file — tell me if you need a PO added”]
Work covered: [one line that matches the SOW]
Pay: [ACH / link / check] — details on the PDF.

I’ll assume this is with AP unless I hear otherwise. Questions on the line items: reply here.

Thank you,
[You]
[Phone]
[Invoice ####]
```

**Why the subject looks boring:** AP searches “invoice” and the vendor name. “Just a quick bill :)” dies in a personal inbox.

## Email 2 — Day 7 unpaid (still polite)

Calendar this when you send Email 1.

**Subject:** `Re: Invoice [####] — [Client] — due [date]`  
(Use reply-all on the original thread.)

```
Hi [Name],

Checking that invoice [####] ($[X], due [date]) reached AP. If it is queued, ignore this.

If you need a W-9, PO on the face, or a split into two lines, tell me what to change and I’ll resend the PDF the same day.

Thanks,
[You]
```

Offer the **blocker**, not a guilt trip. US AP often needs a W-9 once; keep a current W-9 PDF in your admin folder.

## Email 3 — Day 0 of overdue (due date + 1 business day)

```
Hi [Name],

Invoice [####] was due [date] and still shows unpaid on my side ($[X]).

Can you reply with: (1) paid — I’ll watch for it, (2) date it is scheduled, or (3) what’s missing from AP.

I pause non-critical extra work on this job until it’s current. Ongoing scoped work already in motion continues through [date] as discussed.

[You]
```

The pause line only if it is your posted policy. Do not invent a shutdown you will not keep. Do not threaten small-claims in this email.

## Rules

- Send invoices **the day the trigger fires** (milestone or retainer date), not when you “have time Sunday.”
- One invoice per email. Do not stack three months and apologize.
- Never only-DM an invoice in Slack or Instagram. Those are not AP systems.
- If the client pays by credit card and the fee is on them, that must already be in the scope. Do not add 3% in the email as a surprise.

## Drill

Forward your last invoice email to yourself. If the subject would not survive a search for the invoice number, rewrite it and save as a Gmail template / signature snippet.

## Done when

You have Emails 1–3 in a note you can paste. Your invoice PDF fields match the email. A calendar rule exists: “send → +7 days nudge.”
