# Lesson 3 — The weekly update that prevents surprise

**Time:** 20 minutes to template; 10–15 minutes each Thursday  
**Output:** A repeating Thursday (or your chosen weekday) hold + a 5-block email.

## Why weekly, not “when there’s news”

Clients invent stories in silence: you’re slow, you’re over budget, you missed the thing they mentioned in a hallway. A short weekly email is cheaper than a Saturday emergency call.

If nothing shipped, you still send. “Nothing shipped” plus a blocker is information.

## Cadence

- Same weekday, same window (e.g. Thursday 3:30–4:00pm local).
- One email per active job, or one email with headers if two jobs share an approver.
- To: the **approver** named in the scope. Cc: extras they requested. Not the whole company.

Put the hold next to your Friday invoice block if you also run agency-ops-course. Updates Thursday, invoices Friday is a clean US week.

## The five-block email

**Subject:** `Weekly — [Job] — week of [Mon date]`

```
Hi [Name],

1) Shipped this week
- [file/link] — [what “done” means]
- [or: nothing shipped; see blockers]

2) Next week (dates)
- [I will…] by [day]
- [I will…] by [day]

3) Waiting on you
- [thing] — needed by [day] or [consequence: “Thursday send slips to Monday”]

4) Scope / money
- On plan | Change pending | Change approved [one line]
- Invoice [####] sent / due / paid

5) Decision needed (if any)
- [A or B] by [day]

Thanks,
[You]
```

No adjectives. No “hoping to.” If a date is at risk, say it in block 3 or 5, not in a paragraph under the signature.

## First update of a job (kickoff week)

Add two lines at the top:

```
Approver for this job: [them]
Update weekday going forward: [Thursday]
Out of scope reminder: [one line]
```

You are training the channel.

## When the week was messy

Keep the five blocks. Put the apology in one sentence at the top, then the facts.

```
I missed Wednesday’s draft; the new date is Tuesday 2pm. Details below.
```

Do not write a confession novel. Do not blame a tool.

## When they reply in Slack instead

```
I’ll put that in Thursday’s update so we have one thread AP and [approver] can search. If it changes this week’s date, I’ll email today.
```

You can be friendly in Slack. Decisions live in email.

## Metrics you may include (optional, one line)

Hours used vs hours bought, if the SOW is hourly. If the SOW is fixed price, **do not** dump hours unless they asked — it invites amateur procurement to argue your speed.

## Done when

Calendar hold exists for the next four Thursdays. Template saved. You send one real update this week even if you think there is “nothing to say.”
