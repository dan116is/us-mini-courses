# Lesson 2 — Scope creep: pause, price, or decline

**Time:** 45 minutes to install the habit; 5 minutes per incident  
**Output:** A change-request reply you send **before** you do the extra thing.

## Definition

**Scope creep** is extra work that was not in the signed one-pager, asked as if it were obvious. “Can you also add the other location?” “Quick, can you jump on their vendor call?” “The CEO hates blue.”

If you do it for free once, you taught a price of $0.

## The 15-minute rule

If the extra is truly under **15 minutes** and does not change a date or a file someone else must approve, you may do it and log it on the weekly update as “included as a courtesy this week.” Twice in a project is a pattern — the third time uses the change email.

Everything else gets the pause.

## Do not start typing in Slack

Move to email (or your project tool that the approver reads). Creep lives in chat because chat feels unofficial. Your reply makes it official without being hostile.

## Email — Pause and price

**Subject:** `Change request — [Job] — [short name of extra]`

```
Hi [Name],

Happy to look at [the extra]. It is outside the current scope ([one line from the SOW: e.g. “one location, two review rounds, homepage only”]).

Options:
1) Skip it and we stay on the current date and price.
2) Add it as a change: $[X] and [Y] extra business days, [what they get], [what is still out].
3) Swap it for [named unused piece] — same price, new date [date].

I won’t start the extra until you reply with 1, 2, or 3 (and a PO if your AP needs one for option 2).

The original work stays on [current date] unless you pick 2 or 3.

[You]
```

**How to pick $X quickly:** your hourly * honest hours * 1.2 if it will spawn a round of review. If you do not have an hourly, use a small menu: $150 / $400 / $900 for S / M / L extras and never negotiate below the posted menu in the same email.

## When the extra is a “tiny” Slack at 6pm

```
Got it. I’ll put this in tomorrow’s change note so we don’t lose it. If it has to land inside this week’s files, that’s option 2 — I’ll send the price in the morning.
```

You have not said yes. You have not said they are difficult.

## When they say “that should have been included”

Stay on the document, not their feelings.

```
I use the signed one-pager as the source of truth so both sides can plan. [Quoted line] is what we booked. I’m still glad to add it as a change (option 2) or swap (option 3). If the one-pager is wrong, tell me which line to correct for next time — this job stays as signed unless we both agree in writing.
```

If the one-pager **is** actually wrong (you forgot a line they paid for), own it and do the work. This template is not for hiding your mistakes.

## When you must decline

Illegal, unethical, or a date that would blow another client’s WIP.

```
I can’t take [extra] on this timeline without dropping [other promised file]. I won’t do that. I can start it the week of [date] as a separate mini-scope, or refer you to [name] if you have a hard deadline. I’ll keep the original job on track for [date].
```

## Internal log

Every change that was option 2 or 3 gets one line on the weekly update (Lesson 3) so the approver’s boss does not think you invented fees.

## Done when

The pause-and-price email is a template. You have a S/M/L extra menu written down. You have told your contractor (if any) not to accept extras in Slack.
