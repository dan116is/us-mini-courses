# Lesson 1 — Pick a SKU you can finish by Friday

**Time:** 45 minutes  
**Output:** One SKU card: title, buyer, file type, $9 / $19 / $29, “done Friday means.”

## What “first dollar” means here

One stranger (or a weak-tie acquaintance who still pays) sends you $9, $19, or $29 for a **file**. That is the whole win. It is not a business. It is proof you can finish.

If you already sell retainers, skip this course. If you have a 40-page course in your head, this course is how you **cut it** until it fits a week.

## The four filters (all must pass)

1. **You have done the thing in the last 18 months** in the US (or for US rules). No researching a new career.
2. **The buyer already knows they have the problem.** You are not creating a market. Examples: a youth-soccer parent needs a snack-schedule sheet; a new landlord needs a move-in photo checklist; a church volunteer coordinator needs a Sunday greeter script.
3. **The deliverable is a file you can attach to email:** PDF, Markdown, spreadsheet, or a zip of those. Not a login. Not a community.
4. **Friday definition of done** fits in one line: “12-page PDF + 1-page sales HTML, both named and dated.”

If a filter fails, pick a smaller slice of the same knowledge.

## Price: $9, $19, or $29

| Price | Use when |
|---|---|
| $9 | One checklist or one sheet (1–3 pages). Impulse. |
| $19 | A short pack: checklist + email scripts + one example filled in. |
| $29 | A mini playbook: 8–15 pages that someone can run without you. |

Do not pick $29 to “look premium” if you cannot write 8 pages by Thursday. $9 that exists beats $29 that is still an outline.

This course costs $29 because it is the playbook tier. Your SKU can be cheaper.

## Idea killer list (write three, keep one)

Copy:

```
IDEA A
Buyer (job + situation):
Problem in their words:
File they get:
Why I can write this without new research:
Hours to a messy v1 (honest):
```

Repeat for B and C. **Keep the one with the lowest honest hours** that still passes the four filters. Ambition is how this slips to next quarter.

### Good SKU shapes (steal the shape, not the niche if it is not yours)

- “First 14 days as a volunteer coordinator” — timeline + 6 emails
- “Move-in condition photos, apartment edition” — shot list + naming scheme + tenant email
- “Youth rec team: season-start parent packet” — one PDF the coach can forward
- “1099 January folder” — what to keep, what to send a CPA (not tax advice — a filing system)
- “Booth weekend for a craft fair” — packing list, cash box sheet, rain plan

### Bad SKU shapes (this week)

- Anything with “ultimate,” “certification,” or “masterclass”
- A Notion template that requires you to become their IT person
- A topic you know from tweets, not from doing
- A localized product for a country whose rules you do not live

## The SKU card (this is the only brief)

```
TITLE (≤ 70 characters):
BUYER:
THEIR FRIDAY PROBLEM:
FILE TYPE + PAGE COUNT TARGET:
PRICE: $9 / $19 / $29
IN: (bullets)
OUT: (bullets — at least three)
LICENSE: personal use / one team of ≤5
REFUND: file resent if corrupt; otherwise all sales final
FRIDAY DONE LINE:
```

**Out list is mandatory.** Example: “I will not customize this to your league. I will not hop on a call. I will not update it when your state changes a form.” Those sentences keep you from building a consultancy by accident.

## US compliance in one paragraph (not legal advice)

If you sell a download to US buyers: say what they get, the price, that it is a digital file, and how to get help if the file will not open. Do not claim results you have not measured. Do not use other people’s trademarks in the title. If the topic is tax, medical, or legal, label it “organizing / process, not professional advice.” You are responsible for your own sales tax questions with a CPA; this lesson does not compute nexus.

## Done when

One SKU card exists. The other two ideas are crossed out, not “parked in a backlog.” Lesson 2 is writing, not choosing.
