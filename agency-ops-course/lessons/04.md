# Lesson 4 — WIP limits, owner calendar, and who owns which lane

**Time:** 60 minutes  
**Output:** A posted WIP limit, three role cards, and owner “office hours.”

## The failure mode

In a 1–5 person US agency the owner is usually the Commit lane **and** the emergency Deliver lane **and** the only person AP knows. The weekly rhythm collapses because Monday’s plan assumed eight hours the owner does not have.

This lesson is constraints, not motivation.

## WIP limit (work in progress)

**WIP** = jobs in Deliver that are not waiting on an external blocker you already nudged.

Set a number:

| People who do client work | Starting WIP cap (active delivery jobs) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 5 |
| 3–5 | 2 per delivery person, plus 1 shared “quick” slot |

A retainer that takes 2 hours a week counts as **one** job if it is one client. A retainer that is actually five unrelated mini-projects is five jobs. Be honest; the cap exists to make you split or say no.

**Rule posted in the scoreboard header:** “We do not start job N+1 until a job leaves Deliver or we explicitly bump a date with the client.”

When a shiny inbound arrives on Wednesday, the answer is: “We can kick off the week of ____ unless you want to replace job X.” That sentence is how small shops stay paid.

## Role cards (even when one human wears two)

Write these on one page. Names can repeat.

### Owner / operator

- Sets prices and says yes/no to new work.
- Owns the Friday numbers.
- Only person who can break the WIP cap (and must write why on the scoreboard).
- Does **not** silently take every stuck task.

### Delivery lead

- Owns next actions on Deliver rows.
- Runs QA against the SOP quality bar.
- Escalates date slips on Wednesday, not after the client notices.

### Client lead

- Owns Demand first reply and Collect invoices/nudges.
- Is the name on weekly updates.
- Protects the approver list (one approver per job).

If you are two people, typical split: Person A = Owner + Client lead; Person B = Delivery lead. If you are one person, you still write the cards. They tell you **which hat you are wearing in this 25-minute block**. Mixing hats mid-meeting is how Monday Plan becomes a vent session.

## The owner’s calendar (the actual constraint)

Block these recurring holds **before** client calls:

1. The three rhythm meetings (Lesson 3).
2. **Deep work:** 2× 90-minute blocks on days you usually deliver (e.g. Tue 9–10:30, Thu 9–10:30). Label them with a client job, not “focus.”
3. **Office hours for the team / contractors:** 30 minutes Tue and Thu. Slack questions wait for this unless production is down.
4. **Admin:** Friday 2:30–3:25 — invoices, payroll, receipts. Adjacent to Friday Close on purpose.

Everything else is available for sales calls and client workshops. If a prospect can only talk during a deep-work block, offer the next open office-hours-adjacent slot. You are training the market that you have a week.

**Phone:** Delivery deep-work blocks go on Do Not Disturb. Client lead hat can watch the inbox; Owner hat cannot “just peek” without stealing the block.

## Saying no without a speech

Paste-able:

> We’re at capacity on delivery through [date]. I can put you in the Monday Plan for the week of [date], with kickoff that week if the one-pager is signed. If the deadline is immovable, the honest answer is we shouldn’t take it.

Do not add a discount to compensate for the wait. Waiting is the point of a WIP limit.

## Contractors and the rhythm

If a 1099 does delivery:

- They get the SOP page and the scoreboard row, not your whole Drive.
- They join Wednesday Check only (15 min) unless they own a client.
- They do not talk to the client unless the Client lead card says so.
- Their next action is a date on the scoreboard, not a vibe in iMessage.

## Done when

WIP number is written at the top of the scoreboard. Role cards are one page in the same folder as SOPs. Owner calendar has deep work + admin + meetings for the next four weeks.

Lesson 5 is four weeks of running this until it is boring.
